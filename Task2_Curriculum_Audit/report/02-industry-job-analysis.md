## 3. Main findings

1. The baseline already includes many important foundations for AI and data-science work.
2. Python and R are explicitly mentioned in Industry Coding Practice.
3. Machine Learning and Deep Learning are separately listed.
4. Database Management Systems and Data Wrangling and Visualization support data-oriented work.
5. The reviewed postings emphasize turning models into usable and maintainable software, not only training models.
6. APIs, cloud deployment, MLOps, model monitoring, LLM applications, security and responsible AI deserve explicit practical coverage.
7. These are gaps in the visible course-title evidence, not proof that the topics are absent from the actual teaching or detailed syllabi.

## 4. Limitations

The postings are examples of employer requirements, not a statistically representative survey of the entire AI/ML job market. Two postings are experienced-role descriptions; the TE Connectivity posting is titled Data Scientist I. Job requirements can change or expire.

The analysis compares job requirements with visible handbook course titles only. Detailed course outcomes, laboratory hours and assessment methods were not available for verification.
