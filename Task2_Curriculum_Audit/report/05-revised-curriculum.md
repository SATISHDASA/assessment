# Proposed Revised Curriculum — B.Tech CSE (AI & ML)

## 1. Objective

Modernize the visible 2023–24 curriculum baseline to strengthen practical AI/ML engineering, deployment, data engineering and responsible AI skills while preserving the existing programme credit totals as a planning constraint.

This is an illustrative proposal, not an approved curriculum.

## 2. Credit baseline and discrepancy

The handbook page prints a grand total of 161 credits. Its eight semester totals add up to 159 credits. It separately lists a 3-credit summer internship, producing a further unresolved discrepancy.

For planning only, this proposal uses the printed 161-credit figure as the reference. The institution must confirm the authoritative programme total before implementation.

## 3. Proposed credit-change target

A planning target of approximately 20% of the printed 161 credits is:

161 × 20% = 32.2 credits.

For an illustrative whole-credit target, approximately 32 credits would be reviewed or modernized.

This does **not** mean adding 32 credits to the programme. It means reviewing existing credit allocation and using a combination of revised laboratory content, updated assessments, integrated modules and electives.

## 6. Prerequisite and sequencing considerations

The following are proposed academic sequencing checks, not confirmed official prerequisites:

- Programming and Data Structures should precede advanced software and AI application laboratories.
- Probability and Statistics should support Machine Learning.
- Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning foundations should precede advanced Generative AI applications.
- Database and data-processing foundations should support Cloud and Data Engineering.
- Software Testing and Reproducibility should be integrated before the final project.
- MLOps should follow practical model-development experience.

Faculty must verify prerequisite rules and credit regulations before approving any change.

## 7. Regulatory and approval requirements

Before implementation, the institution should:
1. Confirm the authoritative total credits and summer internship treatment.
2. Map proposed modules to programme outcomes and course outcomes.
3. Check applicable UGC, AICTE and university regulations.
4. Verify contact hours, laboratory hours and assessment requirements.
5. Obtain the required departmental, academic council and institutional approvals.
6. Check whether proposed changes affect students already enrolled under earlier regulations.

No claim is made here that any regulator has approved this proposal.

## 8. Implementation approach

**Phase 1:** Update existing laboratories with Git, testing, data validation and reproducibility.

**Phase 2:** Introduce deployment, API and cloud exercises in relevant existing courses.

**Phase 3:** Offer Generative AI, RAG and responsible AI modules as approved electives or integrated modules.

**Phase 4:** Evaluate student projects, employer feedback and faculty workload before further revision.

## 9. Conclusion

The proposed approach retains the visible mathematical, programming and AI/ML foundations while strengthening practical engineering skills. It uses a credit-neutral planning constraint and requires institutional verification of the handbook's credit discrepancy and all applicable academic regulations.
